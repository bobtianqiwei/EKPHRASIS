Before tasks

1. Introduction

Read verbally. Use Show participant card only if you want the participant to read the same text.

Hi, thank you for joining the study today.

We are studying how different forms of feedback may support learning graphic design vocabulary. During the study, you will create simple visual compositions and revise them based on different kinds of feedback.

We are interested in how you understand the design concepts and how you make decisions while designing. We are not evaluating your artistic ability, and there is no single correct composition.

The study will take approximately 40–45 minutes.

During the design tasks, please think aloud. Tell me what you notice, what you are thinking about, and why you make particular changes.

I will share my computer screen and give you remote control through Zoom. You do not need to install anything.

[Complete consent and recording procedure.]

Do you have any questions before we begin?

Wrap-up

14. Final Interview

I have a few final questions.

How, if at all, did your understanding of the design terms change during the study?

Before receiving feedback, were there moments when you knew something could be improved but were not sure what to do? What changed once feedback was available?

Across both tasks, how would you describe the difference between written feedback and comparative visual feedback?

Were there moments when the visual feedback helped you notice something that would have been difficult to understand through words alone?

What most helped you turn an abstract design term into a concrete design action?

Did the feedback help you develop your own interpretation, or did it feel like it was telling you what to do?

Did language affect how easy or difficult it was to understand the terms or feedback?

What would you change about EKPHRASIS or the feedback experience?

Is there anything else you would like to tell us about your experience?

Wrap-up

15. Closing

Thank you. You have completed the study.

This research examines how written and comparative visual feedback may help learners connect abstract graphic design vocabulary with concrete visual decisions.

Please do not share the specific study tasks or feedback materials with potential participants until data collection is complete.

Do you have any final questions?

Thank you again for your time.
