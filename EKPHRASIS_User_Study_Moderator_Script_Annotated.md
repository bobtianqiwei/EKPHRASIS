# EKPHRASIS User Study Moderator Script

Estimated duration: 40–50 minutes

## Moderator interface guide

The Study console contains these controls:

- Context: `Text`, `Visual`
- Task: `Start task`, `Before feedback`, `Feedback 1: [Text/Visual]`, `After revision 1`, `Feedback 2: [Text/Visual]`, `After revision 2`, `Post-task`

Inside a study card:

- Use `Next` to continue to the next prompt.
- Use `Back` to return to the previous prompt.
- After selecting text feedback, `Back` returns to the feedback bank.
- Use `Done` to finish the current button sequence and close the card.
- Use `×` to close a card without finishing the sequence.
- The participant should click a `1–7` button when a rating question appears. If needed, the moderator may click the number stated by the participant.

Important current-interface notes:

- `Start task` contains Pre-task, Definition, and Initial Composition in one continuous card sequence.
- `Post-task` contains Post-task and Compare the Feedback in one continuous card sequence.
- Completing Task 1 through `Post-task` automatically advances the interface to Task 2 and reverses the feedback order.
- The current interface starts Task 1 with Text → Visual and Task 2 with Visual → Text. There is currently no interface control for changing Task 1 to Visual → Text.
- Introduction, Interface Practice, Transfer Task, Final Interview, and Closing have no console buttons. Conduct them verbally.
- The Transfer Task confidence rating is not recorded by the current interface. Record it separately if it is required in the dataset.

## 4. Definition

**[CARD: [TERM] Definition]** Read only the definition for the selected term. Click `Next` when finished.

### Visual Balance

Visual balance refers to how visual weight is distributed across a composition.

Visual weight can come from properties such as size, color, contrast, density, texture, position, or complexity.

A composition feels balanced when its visual weight feels intentionally distributed rather than unintentionally heavy or unstable in one area.

Balance does not require symmetry. Different visual elements can counterbalance one another through their relative weight and position.

### Visual Hierarchy

Visual hierarchy refers to the order in which a viewer notices and understands elements in a composition.

Designers can create hierarchy through properties such as size, contrast, color, spacing, alignment, position, and grouping.

A clear hierarchy establishes different levels of visual importance, helping the viewer understand which elements are primary, secondary, and supporting.

### Visual Rhythm

Visual rhythm refers to a sense of visual movement created through repetition and variation.

Designers can create rhythm through repeated or varied properties such as shape, color, line, spacing, texture, size, or direction.

Repetition can create continuity, while controlled variation can create movement, change, and emphasis within the composition.

## 5. Initial Composition

**[CARD: Initial Composition]** Read the prompt, then click `Done` to close the card before the participant edits the canvas.

Using the provided visual elements, please create a composition that demonstrates [TERM].

You may move, resize, rotate, recolor, duplicate, or delete elements.

You do not need to create a realistic poster or communicate a specific message. Focus on the visual relationships among the elements.

Please think aloud while you work.

You may begin.

## 6. Before Feedback

**[MODERATOR ACTION]** When the initial composition is complete, click `Before feedback`.

**[CARD: Before Feedback]** Use `Next` after every response. Ask the participant to click a `1–7` button for both rating questions. Click `Done` after the final question.

Before you receive any feedback:

What do you think currently works well for [TERM]?

What, if anything, would you consider changing next?

How confident are you that your current composition demonstrates [TERM], from 1 to 7?

Is there anything you currently feel uncertain or stuck about?

## 7. Feedback 1

**[MODERATOR ACTION]** Read the console label. It will show either `Feedback 1: Text` or `Feedback 1: Visual`. Follow the matching instructions below.

6. Click `Next` to continue through the questions.
7. For each rating card, ask the participant to click one `1–7` button.
8. On the revision prompt, click `Done` so the participant can edit the canvas.

You will now receive a short piece of written feedback.

Please read it and think aloud about what it means for your composition.

[Show standardized feedback.]

- How helpful was it for deciding what to do next?
- How much did you trust the feedback?

## 12. Compare the Feedback

**[CARD: Compare the Feedback]** Continue using `Next` after each response. Click `Done` after the final question.

Did one make it easier to decide what to change? Why?

Did either type of feedback show you something the other did not?

Did either one feel more inspiring or more restrictive?

Do you think the order in which you saw the two kinds of feedback affected your experience?

Was there a moment when your understanding of [TERM] changed or became more concrete? What caused that?

## 14. Final Interview

**[MODERATOR ACTION: No study-console button. Conduct this section verbally.]**

I have a few final questions.

How, if at all, did your understanding of the design terms change during the study?

Before receiving feedback, were there moments when you knew something could be improved but were not sure what to do? What changed once feedback was available?

Across both tasks, how would you describe the difference between written feedback and comparative visual feedback?

Were there moments when the visual feedback helped you notice something that would have been difficult to understand through words alone?

What most helped you turn an abstract design term into a concrete design action?

Did the feedback help you develop your own interpretation, or did it feel like it was telling you what to do?

Did language affect how easy or difficult it was to understand the terms or feedback?

What would you change about EKPHRASIS or the feedback experience?

Is there anything else you would like to tell us about your experience?
